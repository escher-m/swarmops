The problem

Agents act. Your audit log doesn't know it.

You already have agents investigating incidents and proposing fixes. What you don't have is a record a compliance lead can read — one that survives the agent vendor changing, and that nobody can quietly edit afterwards.

Traces aren't audit trails

LLM observability tools were built for prompt engineering and evals, not compliance retention. They capture spans and token counts. They don't record which policy rule permitted an action, or who accepted responsibility for it.

A trace tells you what happened. An audit record tells you what was allowed to happen, and by whom.

trace span=tool.invoke  duration=812ms  tokens=1,204
record allowed_by=rule:human_approved_rollback
approver=okta|00u4k2  evidence=4 artifacts
prev_record_hash=ca9c09c4c285…

Evidence expires

The log line that justified a rollback is gone after 30 days of retention. At audit time you can say the agent had a reason. You cannot produce it.

“lgtm 👍” is not evidence

A Slack thread isn't linked to the command that ran, doesn't name the rule that allowed it, and can be edited by whoever wrote it.

And next year you'll run a second agent vendor

Each one keeps its own trail, in its own format, with its own retention. Two vendors, two incompatible answers to a single audit question.

vendor A trace vendor B spans a Slack thread no shared record

How it works

Five steps, one record each

Scroll. The panel keeps up.

1 · Intercept

The adapter subclasses your agent's own tool class and captures the exact command it ran and the exact output it saw.

2 · Capture evidence

Output under 8 KiB is stored inline. Anything larger becomes a blob addressed by the SHA-256 of its own bytes, so it cannot be swapped later.

3 · Evaluate policy

Default deny. Explicit denies beat matching allows. A missing fact fails closed — if we can't tell whether a change is reversible, it isn't.

4 · Gate on a human

The executor re-derives its own authority from the ledger and runs the command read out of the blob the approver actually saw.

5 · Seal the chain

Each record commits to its predecessor's hash. Editing record N breaks every hash after it, all the way to the head.

Append-only, enforced by the database

The application role holds INSERT and SELECT — never UPDATE or DELETE. Triggers reject mutation for every role including the table owner, at statement level so it holds on an empty table. 

bash scripts/prove_append_only.sh

Tampering is detectable anyway

Database controls stop your application and your operators. They cannot stop a superuser. The demo plays the attacker — disables the triggers, stages three attacks, and puts everything back.

runnable

Evidence is immutable, not just logged

Evidence is addressed by the hash of its own bytes. Verification re-hashes every blob against the address its record cites — so rewriting a log excerpt is caught even though the ledger itself was never touched.

Posture

We are not building another AI SRE

The investigation agents are good and getting better. The gap is underneath them: no shared, durable, vendor-neutral record of what they were allowed to do and what they did.

We wrap your agent, we don't replace it

The first adapter targets HolmesGPT, in the CNCF Sandbox since October 2025. The interception point is the one seam every agent framework has: tool invocation. Your agent doesn't know we exist.

Nothing executes without a recorded approval

An execute record is schema-invalid without an approver and a pointer to the approval that authorised it. Silent auto-remediation can't be recorded as compliant, because it can't be recorded at all.
