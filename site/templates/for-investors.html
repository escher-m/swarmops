For investors

Why the audit record becomes infrastructure

Early-stage and technical. The bet we are making, the wedge we are making it through, and the parts that are already built and independently checkable.

The thesis

Agents are moving from answering questions to taking actions. The moment an agent can restart a workload, roll back a deploy or rotate a credential, someone becomes accountable for that action — and today there is no durable, vendor-neutral record of who.

Three forces converge on this. Agents are acquiring write access; the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations reached full enforcement on 2 August 2026, and SOC 2 and the NIST AI RMF increasingly demand attribution at operation level; and enterprises are already running more than one agent vendor, which makes a per-vendor trail structurally unable to answer "what did our agents do last quarter".

The bet is that the record becomes the durable layer, the way the audit log outlived every application that wrote to it. Agent frameworks will churn. What the auditor reads should not.

The wedge

Not "AI governance platform" — too broad, too slow, sold to a committee. The wedge is narrow and technical: one instrumented agent, one incident type, one auditor-ready artifact. A platform team adopts it because their agent just got write access and their compliance lead asked a question they could not answer. It spreads because the second agent vendor produces records in the same shape as the first.

What is built and verifiable today

Everything below is in the repository and checkable by running a command — not a roadmap slide.

The record schema

JSON Schema with conditional rules that make silent auto-remediation structurally unrecordable: an execute record is invalid without an approver and a pointer to the approval that authorised it.

Append-only, provably

Database grants plus statement-level triggers. Ten mutation attempts refused, including as the table owner, in a script a prospect can run themselves.

Tamper detection

Three attack classes — edit a record, delete an approval, swap an evidence blob — staged by a demo that plays the attacker with superuser access. All three caught.

The full loop

Fault injected into a reference service, investigated, proposed, blocked at the gate, approved by an authenticated human, executed, recovery verified by probe, chain intact.

Risks we would raise ourselves

  • Agent platforms absorb this. The most likely outcome. Each has an incentive to own its own trail. Our answer is that a per-vendor trail cannot solve a multi-vendor question — which only holds while enterprises run more than one.
  • Compliance demand may lag capability. Agents get write access before anyone is asked to prove what they did. If the first audit question lands in 2028 rather than 2026, the wedge is early.
  • GRC incumbents extend downward. Vanta and Drata already own the auditor relationship. Adding agent-action evidence is a smaller leap for them than building distribution is for us.
  • It may be a feature. If the record is easy enough to bolt on, it is a library, not a company. The counter-argument is the evidence store and the policy pinning — the parts that are tedious rather than clever.
